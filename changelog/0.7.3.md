summary: Share your camera beside your screen, and see everyone's cameras together.
---

### New

- **Camera and screen together.** The top bar's camera and screen buttons now work on their
  own: starting your camera no longer stops your screen, and the other way round. Viewers see
  your screen with your camera small in its corner.
- **Your camera in the corner.** When you watch someone sharing both, click their camera to
  make it larger, and click again to make it smaller. A button in the footer hides it.
  Watching their camera on its own is also possible: it is offered as a picture of its own.
- **Everyone's cameras at a glance.** When you are not watching anything, the stage shows
  every camera that is on, side by side, with their sound. Click one to watch it; click the
  large picture again to go back. Each camera can be muted from its tile.
- **Mute your microphone.** While you share your camera, a button beside the shared-audio
  controls mutes your microphone for viewers.
- **Android** shows a camera shared beside a screen in the screen's corner (tap it to make it
  larger), and plays both the shared sound and the microphone of the person you choose.

### Improved

- **Double-click to go fullscreen.** On the large picture, a single click goes back and a
  double click toggles fullscreen.
- Going live no longer puts your own preview on the stage. With the preview on, you appear
  among the cameras instead, as in a call. Your own camera looks mirrored to you, as in the
  camera dialog; viewers see it the right way round.

### Good to know

- The cameras on the overview are heard as soon as they appear, before you choose anyone.
  Mute anyone you do not want to hear from their tile. Screens still wait to be chosen.
- Sharing both a screen and a camera uses more upload bandwidth, and watching several cameras
  at once uses more download.
- The stop button in the footer stops your screen first; press it again, or use the top
  bar, to stop the camera. Changing channel or signing out stops both.
- A popped-out broadcast shows the screen without the camera.
- People on an older version see your screen but not your camera while you share both, and
  do not hear your microphone. A camera shared alone still reaches them.
