### Improved

- **Sharing a game with an NVIDIA graphics card is smooth.** With **Settings › Broadcast ›
  Hardware acceleration** on, sharing a window used to fall to a few frames per second while
  you played, and the picture could smear. The window is now encoded on the graphics card and
  sent on the same connection as its sound, so it stays fluid and in step with the audio.
- **A window bigger than the quality you picked is scaled down on the graphics card.** A 4K
  game shared at 1080p now goes out at 1080p, which looks sharper than 4K squeezed into a
  1080p bitrate and costs the graphics card less.
- **Viewers get a picture straight away** when they join a broadcast already running, or after
  a moment of lost connection, instead of waiting a couple of seconds for the next full frame.
- **The status light's details show what is really being sent** while sharing this way: the
  graphics card's encoder and the actual resolution.

### Good to know

- This applies to Windows with an NVIDIA graphics card, when sharing a window with hardware
  acceleration on. AMD and Intel graphics, sharing a whole screen, and macOS work as before.
- The frame rate follows what Windows captures from the game. On a high-refresh display that
  can be below 60 frames per second, for example 48 for a game running at 144 Hz.
